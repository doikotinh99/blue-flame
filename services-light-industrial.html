Industrial Field Prowess

Solving Chicago Industrial Climate Obstacles

How Blue Flame engineering eliminates the common headaches of workshop and warehouse operations.

Rapid Sub-Zero Dock Infiltration

When 14-foot overhead roll-up doors open 30 times a day in -10°F wind chills, hot air rushes out instantly. We install high-velocity gas air curtains above door headers and radiant infrared tube arrays that keep work areas warm regardless of open doors.

Dangerous CO/NO2 Gas Accumulation

Vehicle repair bays require OSHA-compliant high-velocity exhaust removal. We design automated electrochemical CO/NO2 detection arrays that modulate exhaust fans and supply dampers in real-time, safeguarding technician health while conserving heated air.

Dust & Combustible Particulates

Woodworking sawdust and solvent vapors gum up standard open burners and risk flash ignition. We install hermetically sealed, separated-combustion unit heaters that pipe clean outside combustion air straight to the burner box.

Industrial FAQs

Frequently Asked Workshop HVAC Questions

Why are radiant tube heaters so much more efficient for service bays? −
Forced-air unit heaters warm the air, which immediately rises to the ceiling where nobody works. When overhead bay doors open, that expensive hot air escapes in seconds. Infrared radiant heaters work like the sun—they emit infrared rays that warm the concrete floor, toolboxes, vehicles, and technicians directly. Even when the garage door opens, the concrete slab acts as a giant thermal battery, radiating warmth continuously.
What are the OSHA requirements for garage and workshop exhaust ventilation? +
OSHA standard 1910.1000 sets strict 8-hour Time-Weighted Average (TWA) limits for Carbon Monoxide (50 PPM) and Nitrogen Dioxide (5 PPM). In vehicle service facilities, local mechanical codes require a minimum continuous mechanical exhaust of 0.75 CFM per square foot or automated sensor-controlled ventilation that cycles on whenever CO levels hit 25 PPM.
Can suspended gas heaters be installed in woodworking or auto paint shops? +
Standard open-flame unit heaters cannot be used in areas where combustible dust or flammable vapors are present due to NFPA safety regulations. However, we install "Separated Combustion" models that draw 100% of their combustion air through a sealed intake pipe from outside the building, keeping the flame completely isolated from combustible indoor particles.
What incentives does ComEd offer for industrial radiant heaters and destratification? +
ComEd and Nicor Gas offer custom and prescriptive incentives for upgrading from standard unit heaters to low-intensity radiant infrared heaters (often $1.50 to $3.00 per MBH) and up to $500 per industrial destratification fan. As a registered Trade Ally, we calculate and submit all required documentation for instant project credits.
